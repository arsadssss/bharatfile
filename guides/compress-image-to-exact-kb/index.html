Compress Image to an Exact KB Target

Government forms, job portals and university applications don't just cap your file size — many require it to sit inside a band (e.g. 20–50 KB). Too big and the uploader rejects it; too small and the image turns to mush. The reliable approach: compress to the middle of the band (35 KB for a 20–50 KB requirement) — free, no signup, files never leave your device.

Target-size vs quality slider — why it matters

Most compressors give you a quality slider (80%, 60%…). That's guesswork: 60% quality might give you 47 KB on one photo and 190 KB on another — you keep re-trying. DocToolKit's Compress Image has an exact target-size mode: you type “20 KB” and it binary-searches the compression level until the file lands at or just under your number, then honestly reports “Target Achieved” or “Best Achievable”. One click, done.

PNG caveat: PNG is a lossless format, so very small KB targets can't always be hit — the tool will tell you and suggest WebP or JPEG instead. For photos, convert transparent PNGs to JPG first for far smaller files.

Step-by-step: compress to any exact KB

  1. Open Compress Image.
  2. Upload your JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF or SVG file.
  3. Enter your target — e.g. 100 KB for a portal asking “under 100 KB”, 50 KB for a 20–50 KB band, 20 KB for signature limits.
  4. Download. Check the result opens cleanly and text/edges still look sharp.

Pro tips

  • Crop first. Empty background is dead weight — trimming margins with Crop Image before compressing gives visibly better quality at the same KB.
  • Resize if it's huge. A 4000×3000 px phone photo compressed to 20 KB will look bad. Shrink it to the portal's pixel dimensions first with Resize Image, then compress.
  • PNG transparency note. PNGs with transparency convert poorly to JPG. If you don't need transparency, convert to JPG with Convert Image before compressing — JPG compresses far smaller.
